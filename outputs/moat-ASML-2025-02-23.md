# Economic Moat Analysis: ASML Holding N.V.

## 1. Company and context

- **Company:** ASML Holding N.V.
- **Ticker:** ASML
- **Sector / Industry:** Technology / Semiconductors
- **Business:** ASML develops, produces, and services advanced semiconductor equipment—notably **extreme ultraviolet (EUV)** and deep ultraviolet (DUV) lithography systems—used by chipmakers to manufacture leading-edge nodes. It also provides metrology (YieldStar, HMI), computational lithography, and support. The company is the **only commercial supplier of EUV lithography**, which is required for the most advanced logic and memory chips. It operates globally (Netherlands HQ) with ~43k employees and serves TSMC, Samsung, Intel, and others.

---

## 2. Moat rating

**Wide**

ASML has a **wide economic moat** based on a de facto monopoly in EUV lithography, extreme R&D and physics/optics barriers, and very high customer lock-in. ROIC has been **25–35%** over the last five years (key metrics), and gross margins **50–53%** with operating margins **31–35%** (income statements)—well above the cost of capital and consistent with strong pricing power. Morningstar and other analysts assign a wide moat; no competitor offers comparable EUV capability, and the lead is sustained by continuous R&D (R&D/revenue ~13–14%) and deep IP. The moat is durable absent a technological or regulatory regime change.

---

## 3. Sources of moat

| Source | Applies? | Evidence |
|--------|----------|----------|
| **Network effects** | No | ASML’s value is not driven by more users; it’s B2B equipment. Ecosystem benefits exist (suppliers, software) but are secondary. |
| **Switching costs** | **Yes (primary)** | Fabs design entire process flows around ASML tools; switching is not feasible for leading nodes. Long machine life (~30 years) and service ties deepen lock-in. |
| **Low-cost producer** | Partly | Scale and process mastery lower cost per wafer for customers, but the moat is mainly technology and scarcity, not pure cost leadership. |
| **Intangible assets** | **Yes (primary)** | Patents, trade secrets, and decades of optics, metrology, and systems IP. Export controls and “knowledge” restrictions reinforce the barrier. |
| **Counter positioning** | **Yes** | ASML bet on EUV when others (Nikon, Canon) did not; incumbents have been unable to replicate, creating a structural advantage. |

---

## 4. Moat direction

**Stable** (with upside potential to **Widening**)

Margins and returns have improved over the last five years: operating margin from ~31% (2022) to ~35% (2025), ROIC from ~29% to ~35% (key metrics). Demand for advanced nodes remains strong; High-NA EUV adoption extends the technical lead. Export restrictions and China exposure create headline risk but do not yet narrow the moat in the rest of the world. Absent a major technology shift or prolonged demand collapse, the moat is **stable**; if High-NA and services continue to gain share, it could **widen**.

## 5. Supporting data

- **Margins (FY, from income statement):**  
  Gross 50–53%, Operating 31–35%, Net 27–29% (2021–2025).
- **Returns:** ROIC 25–35%, ROE 41–58% (key metrics); ROE 49% (2025, ratios).
- **Balance sheet:** Net cash (net debt negative); debt/equity ~0.14 (2025). Strong FCF; capex covered by operating cash flow.
- **Revenue:** €18.6B (2021) → €31.4B (2025); high single-digit to low double-digit growth.
- **Peers (FMP):** No peers returned; conceptually—Nikon, Canon (lithography), Applied Materials, KLA (different segments) are not direct EUV competitors.
- **FMP rating:** B+ (Neutral); ROE/ROA Strong Buy, P/E/PB Sell (valuation, not moat).

---

## 6. Risks to the moat

- **Export controls and geopolitics:** US and Dutch restrictions limit sales to China; revenue from China may decline (2025–2026). Further decoupling could cap growth in one region but do not remove the EUV monopoly elsewhere.
- **Customer and cycle concentration:** Heavy reliance on TSMC, Samsung, Intel; a sharp downcycle or capex cuts could pressure orders and utilization.
- **Technology disruption:** Any future alternative to EUV (e.g., new lithography or packaging approach) could, over the long term, reduce dependence on ASML; no such alternative is commercial today.
- **Execution and supply chain:** Complex supply chain and long lead times; any sustained failure to deliver or qualify tools could damage customer trust, though switching options remain absent for leading nodes.
