## Moat Assessment

**Moat rating: Wide**

ASML is the sole supplier of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography systems used to manufacture leading-edge semiconductors. ROIC (43%) and ROE (49%) are well above cost of capital; net margin ~29% and dominant share in advanced lithography support a wide, durable advantage.

**Sources of moat**

- **Switching Costs** — Chipmakers (TSMC, Samsung, Intel) have multi-year tool integration, process qualification, and R&D tied to ASML systems; changing supplier is not feasible at the leading node.
- **Intangible Assets** — Deep IP in EUV optics, source, and optics; patents and decades of R&D create a high barrier; no alternative EUV supplier exists.
- **Low-Cost Producer** — Scale in a winner-take-most market drives unit economics and R&D intensity competitors cannot match.
- **Network Effects** — Does not apply in a B2B equipment context.
- **Counter Positioning** — Incumbents (e.g. Nikon, Canon) chose not to pursue EUV at the same scale; ASML’s bet created structural leadership.

**Moat direction: Stable to Widening**

Revenue and EPS growth (3yr CAGR and YoY) are strong; FCF margin and ROIC remain elevated. Demand for advanced nodes and geopolitical onshoring support sustained pricing power and backlog. Risk is execution and cycle, not competitive displacement in the near term.

**Risks to the moat**

- Geopolitical pressure (export controls, local champions) could fragment demand or spur subsidized rivals over the long term.
- Cyclical downturns in semis can compress margins and backlog; timing risk rather than moat erosion.
