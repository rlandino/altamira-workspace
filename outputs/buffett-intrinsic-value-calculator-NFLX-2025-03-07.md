## Owner Earnings Calculation

**Formula:** Owner Earnings = Net Income + D&A − Maintenance Capex ± Working Capital Changes

| Component | FY 2025 (USD millions) | Source |
|-----------|-------------------------|--------|
| Net Income | 10,981 | Income statement |
| + D&A | 16,756 | Cash flow (depreciationAndAmortization) |
| − Maintenance Capex | 688 | Cash flow (capitalExpenditure); total PP&E capex used as proxy |
| − Working capital increase | 456 | changeInWorkingCapital (use of cash) |
| **Owner Earnings** | **26,593** | |

**Assumption:** Total capital expenditure ($688M) is used as maintenance capex. For Netflix, the bulk of D&A is **content amortization** (non-cash); true economic maintenance for a streamer would include ongoing content spend. We follow the formula literally; **free cash flow ($9,461M)** is a more conservative proxy for discretionary cash to equity and would yield lower intrinsic values if used as the base.

**Owner earnings per share:** $26,593M ÷ 4,237.3M shares = **$6.28/share** (using shares outstanding from quote).  
**FCF per share (reference):** $9,461M ÷ 4,237.3M ≈ **$2.23/share**.

## Moat Assessment

**Rating:** **Narrow moat**

| Source | Score (1–5) | Evidence |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Brand power** | 4 | Strong global brand; “Netflix” synonymous with streaming; originals drive recognition. |
| **Switching costs** | 2 | Low: cancel anytime; no contracts. Habit and content library add some stickiness but not structural. |
| **Network effects** | 2 | Weak for content itself; scale helps fund more content and improve recommendations, but not classic network effects. |
| **Cost advantages** | 4 | Scale in content spend and distribution; ability to fund global originals and license catalog. |
| **Efficient scale** | 3 | Large addressable market; scale vs. studios and regional players is an advantage; competition from Disney, Amazon, others limits pricing. |

**Summary:** Narrow moat from brand and content scale. Switching costs are low; competition from deep-pocketed rivals and commoditization of streaming keep the moat from being wide.

## Margin of Safety

- **Current price:** $99.02  
- **Base-case intrinsic value:** $121.00  
- **Discount to base:** (121 − 99) / 121 = **18.2%** (trading at ~82% of base-case IV).  
- **Buffett 30% rule:** Margin is **below 30%**; do not flag as buy on margin of safety alone.

---

## Verdict

**Hold** — Meaningful discount to base-case intrinsic value and narrow moat, but margin of safety is below the 30% threshold. Owner-earnings-based IV is elevated by content D&A; if FCF were used as the base, IV would be lower and the stock could screen as fairly valued or rich. Suited for existing holders; new capital should wait for a larger margin or clearer durability of margins and growth.

---

## Top 3 Permanent Impairment Risks

1. **Content ROI collapse** — If incremental content spend fails to retain or attract subscribers (e.g., saturation, churn to competitors), margins and FCF could fall and the business could be repriced as a lower-multiple, lower-growth streamer.  
2. **Regulatory / licensing** — Major markets imposing strict content rules, licensing cost spikes, or loss of key content rights could permanently reduce appeal and pricing power.  
3. **Key-man / execution** — Heavy reliance on content and product execution; a sustained misstep in strategy (e.g., gaming, ads, pricing) or loss of leadership could erode confidence and multiples before fundamentals recover.
