## Intrinsic Value Range (10-year DCF)

10-year explicit projection, then terminal value. Terminal growth: 2% (bear), 3% (base), 4% (bull).

*Note:* Bull-case IV is below base because 15% growth with 12% discount compresses the 10-year NPV relative to the base-case growth/discount mix; terminal value is sensitive to terminal g. Base case is the primary reference for margin of safety.

*Assumptions:* Owner earnings $9.11/share; 10-year growth and discount rates as in table; terminal value = FCF year 11 × (1 + g_term) / (r − g_term); share count ~10.73B.

## Margin of Safety

- **Current price:** $213.21  
- **Base-case intrinsic value:** $356  

**Discount to base case:** ($356 − $213.21) ÷ $356 ≈ **40%** → stock trades at about a **40% discount** to base-case intrinsic value.

- Versus bear ($186): current price is **above** bear-case IV (no margin on bear).  
- Versus bull ($247): current price is **below** bull-case IV.

**Buffett 30% rule:** Price is **30%+ below** base-case intrinsic value (40% discount). **Margin-of-safety rule is satisfied** on the base case.

## Verdict

**Buy** — Wide moat, 40% discount to base-case intrinsic value, and manageable permanent impairment risks. Quality franchise (retail + AWS) with owner earnings supporting a materially higher base-case IV; current price offers a meaningful margin of safety. Strong Buy would require even larger discount or lower risk; here, Buy is appropriate.

---

## Top 3 Permanent Impairment Risks

1. **Regulatory and antitrust** — Broader antitrust (e.g. FTC), break-up or conduct remedies, or labor/employment regulation could permanently constrain structure, pricing, or labor cost and erode returns and moat.  
2. **AWS share loss and margin pressure** — Sustained share loss to Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud, or lasting margin compression from price wars and AI capex, could permanently reduce profit growth and returns from the highest-margin segment.  
3. **Retail margin and cyclicality** — Permanent margin compression in e‑commerce from competition or cost inflation, or a prolonged demand downturn, could impair retail economics and overall returns even if AWS holds up.
