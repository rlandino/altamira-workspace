## Moat Assessment

**Rating:** **Wide**

| Source | Score (1–5) | Evidence |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Brand power** | 5 | S&P brand is synonymous with credit ratings and indices (e.g. S&P 500); regulatory and institutional trust; 160+ years of history. |
| **Switching costs** | 5 | Ratings embedded in covenants, regulation, and mandates; indices drive trillions in AUM; changing provider is costly and operationally complex. |
| **Network effects** | 4 | Indices and benchmarks become more valuable as more capital tracks them; data/analytics improve with more usage; strong but not purely viral. |
| **Cost advantages** | 3 | Scale in data and analytics; margins are high but competitors (Moody's, Bloomberg, Refinitiv) also have scale. |
| **Efficient scale** | 5 | Ratings (NRSRO) and major index provision are natural oligopolies; few players can replicate breadth (Ratings, Indices, Commodity Insights, Market Intelligence, Mobility, Engineering). |

**Summary:** Wide moat from ratings oligopoly, index entrenchment, and high switching costs. Main risks are regulatory change, loss of NRSRO standing, and index/benchmark substitution over the very long term.

## Verdict

**Hold** — Wide moat and high-quality franchise (ratings, indices, data), but current price is above base-case intrinsic value with no meaningful margin of safety. For a Buffett-style buy, one would require price roughly 30%+ below $352 (i.e. ~$246 or lower). At $452 the market is pricing in growth and quality; suitable for holders, not for new value-style entry on this framework.

## Top 3 Permanent Impairment Risks

1. **Regulatory loss of NRSRO status or severe rating-agency restrictions** — S&P Global Ratings is one of a small number of Nationally Recognized Statistical Rating Organizations. A major regulatory sanction, loss of designation in key jurisdictions, or forced restructuring could permanently reduce the ratings franchise’s value and market share. Precedent: past regulatory and litigation settlements (e.g. post-crisis) show the segment is under ongoing scrutiny.

2. **Structural shift away from third-party indices and benchmarks** — If asset owners and regulators increasingly favor custom or non-proprietary benchmarks, or if passive flows migrate to other index providers or in-house solutions, S&P Dow Jones Indices’ AUM-based economics could be durably impaired. Competition from MSCI, FTSE, and in-house benchmarks is a long-term risk.

3. **Major reputational or governance failure in Ratings** — A high-profile rating failure, conflict of interest, or governance crisis could damage trust in S&P ratings and trigger client defection, regulatory action, or both. The business depends on perceived independence and quality; a permanent loss of confidence could impair the core ratings franchise.
