## Moat Assessment

**Rating:** **Wide**

| Source | Score (1–5) | Evidence |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Brand power** | 5 | Gulfstream in business jets; prime contractor brand in submarines and combat systems; strong government and commercial recognition. |

| **Network effects** | 3 | Limited; supply chain and customer relationships matter but do not create classic network effects. |

**Summary:** Wide moat from long-duration contracts, sole/limited-source positions, and scale in aerospace and defense. Main risks are budget and program execution.

## Margin of Safety

- **Current price:** $363.49  
- **Base-case intrinsic value:** $325  

**Premium to base case:** ($363.49 − $325) ÷ $325 ≈ **12%** → stock trades at about a **12% premium** to base-case intrinsic value.

- Versus bear ($282): current price is **above** bear-case IV.  
- Versus bull ($425): current price is **below** bull-case IV.

**Buffett 30% rule:** Price is **not** 30%+ below base-case intrinsic value (no margin of safety). **Margin-of-safety rule is not satisfied.**

## Verdict

**Hold** — Wide moat and quality defense/aerospace franchise, but current price sits at a modest premium to base-case intrinsic value; no margin of safety for a value buy. Wait for a pullback toward or below base-case IV before adding.

---

## Top 3 Permanent Impairment Risks

1. **Defense budget cut or sequestration** — Sustained reduction in U.S. defense spending or program delays could permanently reduce backlog growth and margins; shipbuilding and aerospace are especially budget-sensitive.  
2. **Major program cancellation or loss** — Loss or significant cut to a key program (e.g. Columbia-class, Virginia, Gulfstream line) could permanently impair a segment and overall returns.  
3. **Execution and fixed-price risk** — Cost overruns on fixed-price or long-duration contracts could permanently compress margins and cash flow; labor and supply chain stress amplify the risk.
